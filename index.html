Case 01

Creative Performance Loop: brief to winner, on repeat

The problem

Paid social rewards creative volume, yet most teams still brief, produce and review ads by hand. Tests run without a clear hypothesis, results sit in Ads Manager, and the next brief starts from opinion instead of evidence. Platforms like Pencil solve this as a product; many teams want the same loop running on their own stack, with their own data and approval rules.

The approach

Treat creative as a product with a release cycle. Every variant carries its hypothesis in its name, every test has a stop rule, and every result is written back as a learning the next brief can use. People approve at two gates; automation does the rest.

How variants get ranked

A single score hides too much, so each variant is read on the metric that matches its job in the funnel: hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) for stopping the scroll, hold rate for the story, CTR and cost per acquisition for the outcome.

Why build it, not buy it

Platform (e.g. Pencil)

Fast to start, strong prediction models trained on large ad datasets, broad model access. You work inside their product and their logic.

Your own loop

Runs in your accounts, your data stays with you (n8n can be self-hosted in the EU), any model can be swapped in, and approval rules match how your team works.

These are not exclusive. Part of the pilot is deciding where a platform fits and where custom automation adds more.

The pilot

Week 1

Audit past creative and data. Agree hypotheses, metrics and stop rules.

Weeks 2–3

Build the loop in n8n. Connect accounts. Dry-run on paused ads.

Weeks 4–5

Run two live test cycles with real budget.

Week 6

Readout, learnings library, handover and team training.

What success looks like

  • Time from brief to live test drops from weeks to days
  • Lower production cost per tested concept
  • A growing share of spend goes to proven winners
  • Every brief starts from a written record of what has worked

Case 02

Aria & Coco: a full animated series pipeline on a solo budget

The brief I set myself

Make a calm, emotionally literate show for young children, and prove that one person with AI tools can produce it to a standard worth pitching to streamers. Aria, a four-year-old, and Coco, a tiny glowing chick, meet in nature to work through big feelings.

Product decisions

  • A repeatable format. 4 seasons × 6 episodes × 5–6 minutes. Every episode has a calming technique, a nostalgia beat for parents and a small “treasure” ritual, so writing and production follow a template.
  • Consistency over spectacle. I locked the character references first and tested tools against them. ChatGPT held the characters most consistently for stills; Kling’s Elements and multi-shot handled motion.
  • A visual style the tools can hold. Soft 3D characters on photographic backgrounds, a “3D storybook” look chosen partly because current models keep it stable across shots.
  • Distribution as a staged launch. YouTube first to learn and build an audience, then pitch to streamers with a 12-slide deck.

Where it stands

Episode 1, “The Leaf That Left”, is finished with voice and sound and published on YouTube. Episode 2 is scripted. An ex-Netflix kids-content mentor gave early feedback on the characters and pointed to music and audio as the next thing to raise, which is now the focus. A picture book based on Episode 1 is in planning.

What it proves

I can take an idea from concept to a released product with AI, make the tool trade-offs myself, and run production on a tight budget. The same pipeline thinking powers the Creative Performance Loop.

Case 03

Luxury fragrance brand: a campaign film made entirely with AI

The brief

I started from an actual luxury fragrance campaign brief: a storyboard, a concept, a location, a character attitude and a lighting direction, the same document a production company would shoot from. The goal was an early visual MVP: show production houses and their clients what AI can deliver against a real brief, before anyone commits budget to a shoot.

How I made it

  • Brief to building blocks. I broke the brief into reusable elements: the hero character, the horse, the landscape and the storyboard beats.
  • Locked elements, then motion. Character and background were fixed first, so every shot kept the same rider, coat and light.
  • Shot by shot from the storyboard. Each storyboard frame became a generated shot in Luma, then cut together into the film.
  • Natural light, real weather. Brooding skies, mist and low sun, matching the brief’s natural-light direction.

Why it matters

Luxury is the hardest test for AI creative because tolerance for off-brand detail is close to zero. A visual MVP lets a client react to the actual film in days instead of approving a treatment on paper, and it sets the quality bar for the generation step of the Creative Performance Loop.
